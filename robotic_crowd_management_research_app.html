1. Executive Summary & Physics Foundations

Understanding the "faster-is-slower" effect and the physical force mechanics behind exit bottlenecks.

In emergency evac scenarios, high desired velocities cause dense crowds to rush towards exit doorways. Physical arches form directly outside the threshold due to high tangential force vectors and contact pressure. The proposed research uses autonomous mobile pillars to actively navigate upstream, dissipate tangential momentum, and organize chaotic flows into capacity-matched queues.

1

Faster-Is-Slower Effect

When panic prompts pedestrians to push harder, lateral contact pressures $f_{ij}^{\text{ped}}$ scale nonlinearly. High tangential momentum $P_\theta(t)$ creates structural jamming arches, halting discharge.

2

The Static Pillar Effect

Fixed obstacles (Helbing & Zuriguel) absorb crowd pressure, creating a low-pressure shadow zone and splitting radial flow into structured streams, improving outflow by 10-50%.

3

Active Robotic Advantage

Static pillars cannot adapt to real-time crowd density shifts or downstream capacity changes $C_{\text{exit}}(t)$. Mobile robotic pillars dynamically re-position to continuously break force chains.

5. Critical Risk Analysis & Technical Mitigations

Addressing psychological panic, hardware constraints, and computational complexity.

Psychological Panic Risk Severity: Moderate

Psychological Rejection & Panic Escalation

Pedestrians in a panic state may perceive mobile robots as blocking obstacles, triggering aggressive pushing or attempts to jump over units.

Technical Mitigation Strategy:
Integrate dynamic visual projection LEDs onto the floor indicating queue paths; wrap robot hulls with compliant soft exterior padding.
Mechanical Friction Risk Severity: High

Mechanical Entrapment & Motor Stall

Extreme compressive crowd pressures ($\rho > 6 \text{ ped/m}^2$) can exceed wheel actuator drive torques, rendering robots immobile.

Technical Mitigation Strategy:
Utilize high-torque holonomic Mecanum wheel drives; design wedge-shaped, low-friction exterior hulls that naturally shed compressive force vectors.
Sim-to-Real Domain Gap Risk Severity: Moderate

Sim-to-Real Policy Transfer Gap

Standard Social Force Models may fail to reflect erratic or irrational human movement during real emergency situations.

Technical Mitigation Strategy:
Apply extensive domain randomization during DRL policy training; fine-tune controllers on empirical human trajectory datasets.
Computation Risk Severity: Low

Curse of Dimensionality in State Space

Multi-robot state spaces grow exponentially under high crowd density, increasing decision latency beyond real-time control limits.

Technical Mitigation Strategy:
Implement ADP value function decomposition; execute centralized training with decentralized policy execution (CTDE).

6. Strategic 4-Phase Research Implementation Roadmap

A structured methodology for advancing from theoretical formulation to empirical validation.

Phase 1 Months 1 - 3

High-Fidelity Simulation & Physics Environment Setup

Build microscopic crowd simulation in Isaac Sim / Gazebo extending SFM contact mechanics, sliding friction, tangential momentum tracking $P_\theta(t)$, and time-varying door discharge $C_{\text{exit}}(t)$.

Phase 2 Months 4 - 6

Hierarchical ADP-MADRL Controller Development

Train upper-level ADP value network $\tilde{V}(S_t; \boldsymbol{\theta})$ for macro-positioning. Train lower-level MAPPO policy for multi-robot obstacle positioning and integrate Control Barrier Functions (CBF).

Phase 3 Months 7 - 9

Comprehensive Multi-Scenario Benchmarking

Benchmark escape times, tangential momentum curves, physical compressive pressure distributions against static pillars and unassisted doorways across uniform, dynamic bottleneck, and asymmetric surge scenarios.

Phase 4 Months 10 - 12

Hardware Prototype & Empirical Validation

Construct omnidirectional mobile robot prototypes equipped with LiDAR and LED floor projectors. Conduct empirical human-robot interaction trials in mock exit corridors.
